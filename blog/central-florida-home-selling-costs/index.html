How Much Does It Cost to Sell a Home in Central Florida?

See a transparent hypothetical seller net sheet and learn which Florida sale costs depend on the property, your agreements, and the purchase contract.

By Patrick Hammon · Coldwell Banker Ellison Realty · Published November 24, 2026

There is no dependable flat percentage that tells every Central Florida homeowner what they will receive after a sale. Your proceeds start with the sale price, then change with the mortgage payoff, deed tax, any agreed brokerage compensation, contract terms, property charges, and costs you choose to incur.

Here is an itemized example so you can see how the pieces fit together. The amounts other than the deed-tax calculation are deliberately hypothetical—not local averages, quotes, or a promise of what a sale will cost.

A hypothetical seller net-proceeds example

Assume a home sells for $400,000 in a Florida county outside Miami-Dade. For this illustration, assume the seller's agreements and contract assign the following amounts to the seller:

Example item Amount How the example treats it
Sale price $400,000 Hypothetical sale price
Mortgage payoff −$250,000 Hypothetical payoff; request a dated payoff from the lender
Florida deed documentary stamp tax −$2,800 $400,000 ÷ $100 × $0.70
Brokerage compensation −$8,000 Assumed only for this arithmetic example; not a standard rate or offer
Seller credit in the purchase contract −$4,000 Hypothetical agreed credit
Title, settlement, and recording charges −$1,200 Placeholder; obtain an itemized estimate
Property-tax proration −$1,200 Placeholder; closing-date and contract specific
Association or estoppel charges −$200 Placeholder; applies only if required and confirmed
Seller-selected preparation or moving costs −$2,500 Hypothetical optional spending
Illustrative amount remaining $130,100 Not a final settlement statement or guaranteed proceeds

The deed-tax arithmetic is the only calculated statutory amount in the table: outside Miami-Dade, Florida's current statute sets documentary stamp tax on a deed at $0.70 per $100 or fraction of consideration. For $400,000, that is $2,800. The example assumes that amount is assigned to the seller; confirm payment and contract treatment with the closing professional for the actual transaction.

The other values are demonstration inputs. In particular, the $8,000 compensation line is not a customary percentage: it represents a hypothetical amount that would appear only if the applicable written agreement and transaction terms provide for it. Compensation is negotiable and not set by law. An offer for a seller to pay any buyer-side compensation is also optional and must be addressed by the relevant agreements.

Which costs depend on your agreements or contract?

  • Brokerage services and compensation: The listing agreement describes the services and compensation you and the brokerage negotiate. Do not assume a standard rate.
  • Buyer credits or other concessions: Include only amounts agreed in the signed purchase contract. A buyer may ask for a credit, but it is not automatically a seller cost.
  • Title, settlement, and recording items: Ask the selected title or closing provider for an itemized estimate and check how the contract allocates the charges.
  • Property taxes and association charges: The closing professional calculates any proration for the expected closing date. Confirm assessments, estoppel certificates, transfer charges, and amounts due with the relevant association or district.
  • Repairs and preparation: Cleaning, repairs, staging, storage, moving, and other improvements depend on your plans and the property. They are not all mandatory closing expenses.
  • Mortgage or other liens: Get current payoff statements for the expected closing date; an account balance is not necessarily the final payoff amount.

Florida deed tax is a useful starting point, but it is not the whole net sheet. It does not account for your payoff, agreement, credits, prorations, association documents, title services, or personal tax situation.

Make your own first-pass worksheet

Start with the expected sale price, then subtract:

  1. Dated mortgage and other lien payoffs.
  2. Deed documentary stamp tax, checked against current Florida law.
  3. Brokerage compensation stated in your signed agreement.
  4. Any seller-paid buyer costs or other credits in the signed purchase contract.
  5. Title, settlement, recording, tax proration, association, and district items confirmed by the closing provider.
  6. Repairs or preparation you choose, plus moving and storage costs.

Keep each item on its own line. That makes it easier to replace placeholders with written figures and prevents counting the same credit or fee twice.
